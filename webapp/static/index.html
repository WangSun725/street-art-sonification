        Interaction logic

        1. Artwork and artist statement

          Upload a mural photo and add an artist statement. The app corrects photo orientation and checks the artwork boundary. The processed artwork supplies the pixels used for color, brightness and musical analysis.

        2. Regions and instruments

          Qwen names and locates visible elements, using the statement to guide what it looks for. SAM 3 finds matching shapes, and SAM 2.1 supplies masks where needed. Qwen checks the categories against the masked artwork. Overlap cleanup gives each covered pixel one region.

          Parts in the same category share a region and its default instrument. Up to nine regions can play. Use the menu under a region card to change its instrument.

        3. A shared musical score

          Tempo
          Grayscale brightness and RGB chroma contribute equally to a color’s tempo score. The app uses the colors named in the statement, counts an emphasized color twice, and uses reference RGB for any missing color. Without named colors, it weights the two largest color groups by area. This gives a starting tempo on a 72–144 BPM scale.
          Key and mode
          The largest colored hue sector sets the root. The 12 sectors map to the 12 chromatic notes, starting with red for C. A tie goes to the brighter sector. A grayscale artwork defaults to C. Mean grayscale at or above 127.5 selects major. Lower values select natural minor.
          Harmony
          Dominant hue and mode choose a four-bar progression in 4/4. All pitched parts share one chord per bar.
          Rhythm and melody
          The proportion of adjacent pixels with a strong grayscale change puts each region in a sparse, medium or dense group. Each instrument draws from its own patterns. The drum phrase ends with a short tom fill in its fourth bar. The lead selects a four-bar melody by how well its accented, held and final notes fit the shared chords, and transposes it to the artwork’s key.
          Chord rhythm
          One chord instrument is drawn for the artwork. Guitar, Keys / Mallet, FM Organ and Pad favor patterns whose attacks align with that instrument in each direction. Each part keeps its own note lengths.

          Use Global System to adjust BPM, root or mode for the whole ensemble.

        4. Interaction and focus

          Mouse

          Move onto the artwork to fade in all region parts on the shared timeline. A clear movement to the right selects arrangement A, and a movement to the left selects B at a bar line. The current region opens its filter. A region card holds focus until you click it again or move onto the artwork.

          Eye tracking

          Calibrate in the windowed view and then in fullscreen. Each view uses its own calibration. Looking at the artwork fades in the ensemble and opens the filter on the region you look at. Clear horizontal gaze motion selects A or B just like the mouse. Looking away fades the ensemble out.

          Scan

          Forward uses A and reverse uses B on the shared clock. Each part picks both phrases from the same density band. B uses a different phrase from A. Filtered backing opens region parts near the scan line. Regions at scan line rounds each crossing to playable bars.

          At a change of scan direction, continuing drums keep the outgoing pattern for one beat. A continuing bass plays the current chord root on that beat, with a short fade between sounds. The outgoing lead fades out, and pads can keep ringing when the chord stays the same.

          Edge density along each four-bar stretch selects how many unassigned instruments play accompaniment. Unassigned drums enter first. During playback, use Select ranges and drag left or right to insert a pass.

          Accompaniment lists the active parts during playback. While paused, click an instrument in that panel to preview its loop.

          Link

          While idle, mouse or gaze direction chooses A or B for the ensemble. During a scan, the path sets the default. A clear gaze movement onto a new region opens its filter and switches that instrument to A (right) or B (left) on the next available bar. When you look away, the instrument returns to the scan's version on a bar line. The mouse still controls filter focus and local effects.

          The ensemble normally fades in over 2 seconds and out over 3 seconds. The focus filter opens from 900 Hz toward 20 kHz, making the part clearer.

        5. Local color and effects

          Mouse and gaze sample a circle within the region mask. Scan samples the current pixel column separately for each region. The median red, green and blue values give a representative color, which is converted to HSV.

          Hue
          Continuously blends the left eighth-note delay and right quarter-note delay. Red is fully left and cyan is fully right. The Delay knob sets the total send.
          Saturation
          More saturated colors deepen tremolo at two volume pulses per beat.
          HSV value
          Darker colors send more of the part to reverb.
          Brightness contrast
          A bigger difference between the 90th and 10th percentiles of sampled pixel brightness increases the chorus send.

          Neutral colors and missing samples use the instrument’s manual settings.

        6. Web audio and visual feedback

          Each new artwork draws two FM sounds for every instrument and drum piece. Arrangement A and B use their own sounds, switched on the same bar line as their phrases. Save Preset stores both sound sets.

          The browser schedules notes on a shared clock and plays them through a four-operator FM engine. If no region plays Drums, the ensemble adds a synchronized drum track. Each instrument has its own patch, volume and effects controls. Contours and cards show which regions have focus. Live FM and Effects shows their current controls.
